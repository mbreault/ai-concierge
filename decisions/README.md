# Decision log

Recorded and updated 2026-09-14. This log explains the evolution of the offering; it does not turn model recommendations into approved prices or client commitments. **User-selected** records an explicit choice: AI Concierge branding and an initial paid call below $1,000. **User-supported direction** records agreement about hardware liability and substantially higher pricing. **Proposed** means the current draft favors a detail the user has not selected, including the specific $750 entry price. **Held** means an idea or term remains unresolved.

Read the [comparison](../offerings/comparison.md) beside the [current AI Concierge v4](../offerings/versions/2026-09-14-ai-concierge-v4.md). The [post-Claude synthesis v3](../offerings/versions/2026-09-14-synthesis-v3.md) remains historical. The [user's positioning and entry feedback](../brainstorming/2026-09-14-positioning-and-entry-feedback.md) explains the latest revision. Source references below point to preserved historical documents; their market claims have not been independently reverified in this repository work.

## D001 Entry pricing

**Status: User-selected initial paid call below $1,000; specific price and format proposed.** The [original](../offerings/versions/original-v1.md) offered a $100/30-minute assessment and $200/60-minute tools session. [Gemini](../research/sources/gemini-analysis.md) proposed replacing these with a $950 diagnostic. [ChatGPT after Gemini](../research/sources/chatgpt-discussion.md#turn-2) proposed a $750 diagnostic as an initial launch step, then higher prices after case studies. [Claude v2](../offerings/versions/claude-offerings-v2.md) proposed a free 20-minute fit call, $500/90-minute tools session and $2,500 audit; [ChatGPT after Claude](../research/sources/chatgpt-discussion.md#turn-4) preferred that entry path, which appeared in v3.

**2026-09-14 user correction:** The user rejected $2,500 as the initial call price and explicitly required that the initial call remain **below $1,000**. V4 restores a **proposed $750, 60–90-minute Workflow Diagnostic**, drawn from the earlier ChatGPT discussion. The user chose the ceiling, not the exact $750 figure. The free 20-minute fit call and $500/90-minute tools session remain proposed options.

**Current rationale:** Make the first paid engagement accessible and focused on one meaningful workflow, with a written recommendation. The deeper $2,500 audit stays in the historical proposals and is off the current entry menu; it is not a required first purchase.

## D002 Paid discovery and credit

**Status: Proposed $750 diagnostic with 30-day full build credit; exact credit terms held.** The original called its short assessment “paid discovery” without a written deliverable or credit. Gemini proposed a roadmap and **$950 credited toward a build within 30 days**. Post-Gemini ChatGPT proposed **$750/30 days**, plus a separate $2,500–$5,000 opportunity sprint. Claude v2 and the final ChatGPT discussion moved to **$2,500 Workflow Audit, credited in full toward a build started within 90 days**; v3 preserved that proposal.

**2026-09-14 update:** Following the user's entry-price correction, v4 returns to the earlier **$750 diagnostic, credited 100% toward a build started within 30 days** as an editorial proposal. The user has not selected a credit amount or deadline. The $2,500/90-day deeper audit remains historical, off the current entry menu.

**Current rationale:** The first paid session should identify the next useful step for one workflow and leave a written recommendation, without inheriting the deeper audit's interviews, prototype or report-length commitments. Credit can ease the move to implementation, but is not a proven conversion rate. The [Claude research](../research/sources/claude-market-research.md) includes a no-credit assessment, so its broader “every serious offer” claim should not be adopted literally. See D010 for unresolved credit mechanics.

## D003 Positioning

**Status: User-selected AI Concierge; alternative umbrella rejected.** The original heading was **AI Concierge**, with direct copy about finding a painful workflow, helping people use tools and building the solution. Gemini emphasized senior software engineering, integration and operational reliability. Claude research identified competing hospitality/customer-service uses of “AI Concierge” and proposed **Find it. Build it. Run it.**; final ChatGPT adopted that umbrella in the historical v3 synthesis.

**2026-09-14 user correction:** The user explicitly preferred **AI Concierge** and rejected the proposed “Find it. Build it. Run it.” umbrella. V4 restores AI Concierge as the main name. The rejected alternative remains visible only in historical sources and comparison.

**Current rationale:** The chosen name communicates the personal service relationship the user intends. Preserve the direct voice and explain the engineering and integration work underneath that name. Research observations about other uses of “AI Concierge” do not override this choice. Dedicated offer names such as AI Advisor remain proposals; Concierge is no longer restricted to a premium tier.

## D004 Hardware

**Status: User-supported direction; special-offer prices and terms proposed.** Original Chief of Staff: **$1,500 + $200/month**. Gemini recommended removing hardware entirely because physical devices add onsite, network, peripheral and maintenance obligations. Post-Gemini ChatGPT kept a premium demo/special-offer concept off the launch menu, initially suggesting **$7,500 + $750/month**. The [user then agreed](../research/sources/chatgpt-discussion.md#turn-3) that hardware carried substantial liability and should cost about ten times the original price. Claude v2 later proposed **$2,500 + $300/month**, which final ChatGPT explicitly rejected as still too low.

**Current rationale:** Keep physical Executive AI/Chief of Staff deployments **off the core launch menu**, available only by special scope at **$15,000+ deployment + $1,000–$2,500/month** as a discussion range. An earlier ChatGPT hardware reply used $1,500–$2,500/month; no exact monthly price has been approved. Price the deployment, integration, training and ongoing responsibility, rather than benchmarking a bespoke service against a standardized appliance.

Client-purchased or separately billed hardware, manufacturer warranty, remote support by default and separately priced onsite work were proposed guardrails. State whether processing is local or cloud-based. A physical box alone does not establish local processing or privacy benefits.

## D005 Integration and engineering prices

**Status: Proposed.** Original: **$500 + $100/month** model connections, **$5,000** Copilot agent and **$200/hour** development. Gemini proposed **$4,500** integrations, **$7,500–$12,500** Copilot and **$250/hour** alongside fixed sprints. Claude v2 proposed **$1,500 + $250/month** connections, **$5,000–$12,000** Copilot and **$225/hour in 20-hour blocks**. Final ChatGPT favored higher technical pricing: **$3,500–$5,000+** integrations, **$7,500–$15,000+** Copilot and **$250–$300/hour equivalent**, selling fixed-price sprints where practical.

**Current rationale:** A production connection can involve authentication, permissions, data cleanup, retries, logging and ongoing responsibility. Scope and acceptance criteria should determine the quote. ChatGPT retained $1,500 only as an internal floor for exceptionally small work, not a headline product. Provider subscriptions, usage and hosting should be disclosed separately and billed to client accounts where practical; historical Microsoft price figures need fresh verification before a live quote.

## D006 Workshops and acquisition channel

**Status: Proposed.** Original: **$500/seat public workshop** and **$15,000 company half-day**. Gemini proposed a **$3,500 SMB workshop** and **$10,000–$12,500 executive intensive**. Post-Gemini ChatGPT's final launch table used **$5,000 team / $12,500 executive**. Claude v2 proposed **$350/seat**, **$2,500–$4,000 chamber/association sponsor fee**, **$4,500 company workshop**, and **$15,000 workshop plus shipped agent**. Final ChatGPT favored the sponsored channel and **$15,000+ workshop plus first production build**.

**Current rationale:** A chamber or trade association can provide the audience, venue and existing relationships. The workshop becomes a route to scoped audits and builds. The research's $550 university example suggests the original seat price was not inherently implausible; filling seats against subsidized training was the larger concern. Sponsorship pricing and downstream conversion remain hypotheses to test. A $15,000+ package must buy implementation as well as training; the exact build and delivery dependencies need scope.

## D007 Retainers

**Status: Proposed; delivery allowances held.** Original **“Enterprise” at $1,000/month** bought two 45-minute sessions. Gemini separated **$1,500 advisory**, **$4,500–$5,500 managed systems**, and **$8,500–$12,500 fractional CAIO**. Post-Gemini ChatGPT's final table used **$1,500 Office Hours / $3,000 Advisor / $5,000+ managed systems**. Claude v2 proposed **$400 group Office Hours / $2,500 Concierge**, with four build hours in Concierge. Final ChatGPT preferred **$500 group Office Hours / $3,500 dedicated Advisor / $5,000+ Managed AI Systems**, with **$7,500–$12,500 Fractional AI Lead** as a future offer.

**Current rationale:** Separate group learning, dedicated advice and hands-on system ownership so prices correspond to different work. Avoid “Enterprise” for a small advisory package. Claude's apparent market gap is an interpretation of selected public asking prices, not proof that no engagements transact in that range. Included hours, response access, capacity, overlap between plans and small-build support pricing remain unresolved; the synthesis does not automatically inherit Claude's four build hours or eight-client cap.

## D008 Platform Adaptation

**Status: Proposed category; price and allowance held.** The original promised break/fix and excluded new work. Gemini separated model drift and upstream changes from defects and allocated maintenance to a retainer hour bank. [Claude research](../research/sources/claude-market-research.md), section 4.4, named the third category **Platform Adaptation**; final ChatGPT expressly supported it.

**Current rationale:** Distinguish (1) correcting a failure to meet the agreed specification, (2) adding requested functionality, and (3) restoring existing functionality after provider/model/API changes. The name is Claude's drafting recommendation, not a verified industry-standard clause or proof of a unique service.

Claude v2 proposed two adaptation hours per month at a $250/month Care minimum. The synthesis leaves the allowance and support fee open: two hours at the proposed engineering rate already exceeds that fee before monitoring or triage. Define coverage, caps, billing approval and escalation before selling the support package.

## D009 SLA

**Status: Proposed operating targets; final contract details held.** Original: **48-hour email**, without severity or coverage definitions. Gemini proposed P1 **1–2 business hours** plus **4–8-hour workaround/fix targets**. Claude v2 proposed P1 **4 business hours**, P2 **1 business day**, P3 **3 business days**, **Monday–Friday, 8am–5pm Eastern**. Final ChatGPT preferred the latter structure for a solo consultancy.

**Current rationale:** Define response as human review, an initial assessment and an estimate/next step. Publish response targets separately from system-specific resolution commitments. Do not promise third-party platform uptime or implicit 24/7 coverage. Holidays, reporting channels, clock pauses, outage handling and escalation still need definition.

**Correction to the authored synthesis:** Under the stated 8am–5pm window, a P1 arriving Friday at 4:30pm is due **Monday at 11:30am**, assuming Monday is a covered business day. Friday contributes 30 minutes and Monday the remaining 3½ hours. Claude's historical “Monday noon” example is preserved unchanged in the source. Its automatic 10% response-miss credit, capped at 30% monthly, remains a proposal rather than an adopted commitment.

## D010 Guarantee and unresolved commercial terms

**Status: Held.** Original and Gemini had no 10-hour guarantee. Claude v2 proposed **“10 recoverable hours a month or you don't pay.”** Final ChatGPT saw this as a possible early case-study experiment, not an evergreen guarantee.

Before offering it, define eligible clients, baseline and estimation method, whether “recoverable” means identified potential or realized savings, client dependencies and the refund/fee-waiver procedure. Keep it off the unconditional current offer until those choices are made.

Other open terms to resolve in the next revision:

- Diagnostic credit: whether to adopt the proposed full $750 credit/30-day window, when the clock starts, what counts as build commencement, the applicable invoice, and whether any tools-session credit stacks. The historical $2,500/90-day audit is off the current entry menu.
- Diagnostic/build scope: the written recommendation and boundaries of a 60–90-minute session; acceptance criteria and delivery dependencies for builds. A deeper audit's interviews, prototype or two-week delivery promise would need separate scope if revived.
- Support: small-system plan, monitoring scope, adaptation allowance, overage authorization, warranty/launch-support transition and whether recurring support is required.
- Retainers: included work, minimum term, cancellation, unused hours, capacity and response access. Claude's proposed three-month minimum, 30-day notice and non-rollover terms are not approved defaults.
- Remedies and hardware: final response-credit language, onsite/travel pricing, warranty boundaries and the precise recurring hardware-support price.

Future changes should add a dated note to the relevant decision and create a new offering version. Keep source snapshots and prior versions unchanged; record which proposal was superseded and why.
