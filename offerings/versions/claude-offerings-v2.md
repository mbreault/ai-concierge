# Find it. Build it. Run it.

Sit with someone, find the workflow that hurts, get them using the tools, then build the thing that runs it — and stay on if they want.

> *Notes in blockquotes are for you, not the client. Strip them before this goes anywhere.* *Rationale for every number is in the research report, §5.*

---

## 0\. First call

20 minutes, free. Not a product. Enough to find out whether there's a workflow worth paying me to look at.

> *Your old $100 / 30-minute assessment cost more in scheduling than it earned, and 30 minutes can't produce anything a client can act on. Make it free, make it short, and let it sell the audit. If it turns into a time sink, put $250 on it and credit that toward the audit too.*

## 1\. Find it

### Workflow Audit — $2,500. Two weeks.

One business, one fortnight, fixed scope. I sit with the people who do the work, not just the owner.

You get:

- **A workflow map** of how the work actually moves today — including the parts nobody documented  
- **A ranked opportunity list**, scored on hours recovered × how hard it is to build  
- **Named tools**, not categories. "Claude, for this, because of that" — not "consider an LLM"  
- **A working prototype** of the top candidate, so you can see the thing before you buy the thing. It's a prototype, not a production system, and I'll say so out loud  
- **A 30/60/90 plan** with a name against every line  
- **A written document**, 15–25 pages. Not a deck  
- **A 60-minute readout** with whoever needs to sign off

**Credited in full** against any build started within 90 days.

**If I can't find you 10 recoverable hours a month, you don't pay.**

> *This is the highest-leverage change on the whole sheet. Every credible paid-discovery offer in the market credits forward with a deadline — 30, 60 or 90 days — and the deadline is what converts. The guarantee is how you bridge having 30 years of credibility but no AI case studies yet.* *$2,500 is deliberate: it's below most owner-operators' card limit, so it never touches a procurement process.*

### Tools Session — $500. 90 minutes. One person.

Claude, ChatGPT, NotebookLM — on their actual work, not a demo. They leave dangerous enough to start noticing what should be automated.

Credited toward the audit.

> *Never sell a single hour. Nobody who charges well does. This is a 90-minute block with a specific outcome, and it's a feeder into the audit.*

## 2\. Teach it

The team version. A room full of people, and the first build reveals itself when someone elbows a coworker and says "we could do this for our quoting process."

### Public workshop — $350/head. 4 hours. Minimum 12\.

Or run it **through a chamber of commerce or trade association**: flat **$2,500–$4,000**, they fill the room and handle the venue.

> *Two things. The price is fine on its own — a university charges $550 for a comparable half-day. But you're competing against free: the SBDC's national AI program (funded by a $10M Google.org grant) gives this content away to any small business, and chambers run full-day summits with lunch for $225.* *So don't fight for seats. Go through the chamber. They already have the list, the room, and the trust. It's the cheapest customer acquisition available to a solo operator, and the research shows chambers are actively running these events right now and looking for people to run them.*

### Company workshop — $4,500. Half day, up to 15 people.

Hands-on. Get them on Claude, build one skill together, ask what eats hours of their week. Walk out with the first agent identified.

### Workshop \+ First Agent — $15,000. 30 days.

The half-day above, then I ship the first agent within 30 days, plus 60 days of support once it's live.

> *Your original $15,000 for a half day was the most out-of-band number on the sheet — published private half-days run $999 to $5,000, and the next rung is $75,000 for brand-name firms. But the fix isn't a discount, it's changing what $15,000 buys. A workshop plus a shipped thing is defensible to any owner. A half-day of talking isn't, not yet.*

### What Microsoft bills you directly

Not my fees. Yours, on your tenant.

- **$0.01 per message** pay-as-you-go, or **$200/month for 25,000 credits**  
- A generative answer burns **2 credits** (\~$0.02). An agent action burns **5**  
- **Voice burns 35 credits a minute — about $0.35/min.** This is the line that surprises people  
- Microsoft **disables your agent at 125% of prepaid capacity**, so we'll size the pack deliberately  
- **If your staff already have Microsoft 365 Copilot licenses, most internal-facing agent traffic is free.** Worth checking before we design anything

>   
> *Put this on the sheet in writing. Otherwise you either eat it or have an unpleasant conversation in month two. And lead with the last bullet — it's genuinely good news and most SMBs don't know it.*

### On the AI Chief of Staff

> *Decide what this actually is and say it plainly. A Raspberry Pi can't run a model anyone will find useful. If it's a thin client onto Claude or GPT, that's fine — it's cheap, it works, and the value is that it's always on and you keep it useful. But a buyer who hears "local AI box" assumes privacy benefits, and you don't want to discover that mismatch at install.* *For reference: PrivateOffice sells a genuine local appliance at $4,900 \+ $99/month for \~15 people. Your $2,500 \+ $300/month is a third of their hardware at three times their support — which is the right shape for a service business, but the $300 has to be visibly earned. The Care terms below are what earns it.*

## 4\. Run it

### Care — included with every build. $250/mo minimum.

Break/fix, plus the AI-specific work nobody else has a name for.

**What's covered**

- **Fixes** — anything that doesn't work as specified. Bug fixes, patches, corrections  
- **Platform Adaptation** — changes needed to keep what you have *working* when a model gets deprecated, an API version breaks, or output drifts. Up to **2 hours a month**, then hourly  
- Monitoring, and telling you before you notice

**What's not**

New features, new workflows, new integrations, anything the system was never specified to do. That's a new build or an hourly block.

> *Platform Adaptation is your wedge. I looked for published contract language covering model deprecation, API breaks and prompt drift and found none — not in MSP templates, not in software maintenance agreements, not in any AI vendor's terms. It's neither a bug (it worked as specified) nor a project (the client asked for nothing), so it falls in a gap everyone will eventually hit.* *Naming it does three things: it's the concrete answer to "what am I paying $250/month for," it stops the argument before it starts, and it's the part of the pitch nobody else can make.*

**Response times** — Monday–Friday, 8am–5pm Eastern

|  |  | Response |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **P1** | It's down. Nobody can use it | **4 business hours** |
| **P2** | Badly degraded, or a workaround exists but it hurts | **1 business day** |
| **P3** | Minor, cosmetic, or a question | **3 business days** |

**Response means** I've looked at it, told you what I think is wrong, and given you an estimate. Not an auto-reply.

**The clock runs during business hours only.** A P1 that lands Friday at 4:30 is due Monday at noon. Saying so now beats arguing about it then.

**Resolution times are scoped per system** when we sign, because what "fixed" means depends on what broke and who else's platform it broke on.

**No uptime guarantee.** I don't own the substrate. If Anthropic or Microsoft is down, your agent is down, and no promise from me changes that. Scheduled maintenance and third-party outages are excluded.

**If I miss a response target**, you get 10% of that month's fee back, automatically, without asking. Capped at 30% of a month. That's the whole remedy.

> *Every part of this is load-bearing.* *4 business hours for P1 is honest for a solo operator — it matches what a real software vendor charges a premium for. Don't promise 15 minutes; that's an MSP with a staffed desk.* *Defining "response" as a diagnosis and an ETA, rather than an acknowledgment, is the single most client-friendly thing on this page and costs you nothing but discipline.* *No uptime commitment is correct. The leading MSP contract template doesn't have one either, and neither do most software vendors. Offering one would be a promise about Microsoft's infrastructure, not yours.* *The automatic credit is cheap — 10% of $250 is $25 — and it will make you look more serious than firms charging ten times as much, because the two most-used free MSP templates have no remedy clause at all.*

### Office Hours — $400/mo

A group call once a month with all the clients on this tier, plus email between. Ask anything. Watch what other people are building.

3-month minimum, then 30 days' notice.

> *This is the tier that scales. One call serves everyone on it, so the marginal cost of the tenth client is zero. It's also where the $1,000 clients you were about to sign should land instead — see the next note.*

### Concierge — $2,500/mo

The standing slot where the next agent gets scoped.

- Two 45-minute sessions a month  
- Email and async between, **same business day**  
- **4 hours of build time included**  
- Roadmap refreshed every quarter

3-month minimum, then 30 days' notice. Hours don't roll over — but if you use less than half in a month, I'll book a planning session on me.

**I cap this at 8 clients.** When it's full, it's full.

> *Your $1,000/month "Enterprise" tier sat in a genuine dead zone. The market is bimodal: $800–1,900 is leveraged group delivery, $3,500+ is dedicated one-to-one, and almost nothing transacts in between. You were selling dedicated 1:1 time at a group price and calling it Enterprise, which reads as not-serious to exactly the buyer the word is meant to attract.* *Splitting it fixes both ends. $2,500 is the stretch for a $1M–$20M owner-operator; the market's dedicated tier really starts at $3,500, so test upward once you have two or three case studies.* *The sub-50%-utilization planning session is stolen from the one firm in the market that publishes a policy on unused hours. Everyone else stays silent and buyers notice. It kills the rollover liability and defuses the "I paid for nothing" complaint in one line.* *The client cap is honest — you're one person — and every solo operator at this price point publishes one. Free urgency.*
