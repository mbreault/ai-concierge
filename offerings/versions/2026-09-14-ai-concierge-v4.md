Version: AI Concierge v4 · Date: 2026-09-14 · Editor: Codex, incorporating Mike’s latest feedback.

**Status: current working proposal.** Mike explicitly chose **AI Concierge** and required the initial call to cost **less than $1,000**. The specific $750 diagnostic below is a proposed implementation of that limit, restored from the earlier ChatGPT discussion; the other prices and commercial terms remain proposals. Prices are USD.

Sources: [Mike's latest feedback](../../brainstorming/2026-09-14-positioning-and-entry-feedback.md), [post-Gemini diagnostic proposal](../../research/sources/chatgpt-discussion.md#turn-2) and [post-Claude discussion](../../research/sources/chatgpt-discussion.md#turn-4). The previous [synthesis v3](2026-09-14-synthesis-v3.md) remains unchanged for comparison. This is an iteration document, not a published rate card or signed service agreement.

Sit with someone, find the workflow that hurts, get them using the tools, then build the thing that runs it — and stay on if they want.

Thirty years of consulting and software development, applied to making AI useful inside the systems your business already runs. The work includes data, permissions, integrations, testing and the judgment to know which steps need predictable software or a person.

## Start

**Fit call — free, 20 minutes.** Find out whether there is a workflow worth investigating and whether we are a good fit.

**AI Workflow Diagnostic — $750, 60–90 minutes.** One meaningful workflow. Talk through how it works today, where time is lost, what systems it touches, and whether a practical improvement is worth pursuing. Leave with a short written recommendation and a clear next step. Agree the workflow and preparation in advance so the diagnostic stays bounded.

**Proposed credit: apply the full $750 toward a build started within 30 days.** This restores the earlier diagnostic proposal. Mike has set the price ceiling, but has not selected the exact fee or credit terms. Define the credit clock and qualifying build in the quote.

**Tools Working Session — $500, 90 minutes, one person.** Work with AI tools on a real task and identify the next useful workflow. This is an alternative starting offer for hands-on tool help, not a prerequisite to the diagnostic. Any credit or stacking rule remains open.

The $2,500/two-week Workflow Audit with a prototype, map and roadmap remains a historical deeper-discovery proposal. It is not the entry offer in this version. If a workflow needs extensive interviews or investigation, scope that next engagement after the diagnostic; this draft does not automatically impose a $2,500 audit.

The “10 recoverable hours/month or you don't pay” guarantee remains a **held idea** from the deeper-audit proposal. It does not apply to this diagnostic by default.

| --- | --- | --- |

| New work | A new feature, workflow, integration or materially expanded behavior | Separate quote or agreed engineering capacity |

### Proposed response targets

Coverage proposal: **Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern**, with holidays, absence coverage and escalation channel to be specified before sale. These targets would apply only to systems on an agreed support plan.

| Severity | Example | Initial response target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | System down; nobody can use the supported workflow | 4 business hours |
| P2 | Material degradation or a burdensome workaround | 1 business day |
| P3 | Minor defect, cosmetic issue or question | 3 business days |

Response means a human assessment, a stated next step and an estimate where possible. An automatic receipt is not the response. Resolution depends on the incident and third-party dependencies; do not offer a universal resolution time or guarantee uptime of Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic or other external infrastructure.

For clarity, four business hours from Friday at 4:30 p.m. ends Monday at **11:30 a.m.** under this proposed window, assuming Monday is a covered day. This corrects the arithmetic in the preserved Claude source.

Response-credit remedies, business-day counting rules, planned maintenance, third-party outages and client-caused incidents remain contract-design questions. Claude's automatic 10% credit capped at 30% is an alternative to assess, not an adopted commitment.
