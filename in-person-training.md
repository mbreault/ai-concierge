My friend Mark spent seven years building a B2B SaaS company. He raised over $8 million and got it to a $500K annual run rate.

​

Then six months of part-time AI consulting passed that run rate entirely from working only nights and weekends.

​

So he wound the company down and went all in on AI services.

​

One of the offers driving that growth is in-person AI training, and after unpacking his whole model on the Build With AI podcast this week, I think it's one of the most underrated foot-in-the-door offers in AI services right now.

​

One of my biggest takeaways was this:

​

The workshop is not the product. It's a paid lead magnet.
​

Mark's first public workshop was $495 a head for 4 hours (he was nervous nobody would pay). He marketed it with an Eventbrite page and some LinkedIn DMs, and 15 people showed up.

​

Most everyone who showed up was age 45 and up, by the way. Business owners who are embarrassed to ask about AI at work and are quietly paying to catch up. That demand is everywhere.

​

The public workshop is just the bottom rung. Here's Mark's ladder with real numbers:

​

Free artifact: a skill, an audit, a collection of assets
Workshop plus assessment: $15K to $25K for half a day to two days with a company's team
Custom build: $25K to $50K, delivered in 3 to 6 weeks
Retainer or the next build after that
​

His six-figure proposals are the whole ladder in one package: assessment, workshop, training, build, maintenance.

​

And the build reveals itself during the workshop. Mark watches for one moment: somebody elbows a coworker mid-session and says "we could do this for our quoting process." Three other heads nod. He writes it down. That's the first build he pitches.

​

A workshop is a paid discovery call with 20 people in the room.
​

Two pricing rules worth stealing:

​

1. Work backward from the value. Estimate the low, mid, and best case outcome of what you're teaching, then charge around 20% of that. A workshop for a 150-person company is worth multiples of one for independent contractors.

​

2. Keep public workshops around $500 a head. You want people walking out saying "that was a bargain" and recommending you to everyone they know. You can make mistakes at $500. You can't at $2,000.

​

Want to test this without an audience or a technical background? Here's the play:

​

Step 1: Niche down to the industry you already know. AI for realtors, for roofers, for Amazon sellers. Domain knowledge plus AI knowledge is where the value multiplies.

​

Step 2: Book a room, put it on Eventbrite at $500 a head, and collect payment up front. If two people show up, you made money and those two get the best workshop of their lives.

​

Step 3: Don't overteach. Most attendees' entire AI experience is typing into ChatGPT. Get them set up with Claude, build one skill together, schedule one task, and ask the only question that matters: "What eats hours of your week that you hate doing?"

​

Step 4: When someone comes up afterward and asks if you can do this for their company, say yes. Be honest that it's your first one and trade a discount for a case study.

​

This is already showing up in my own life. Last week my realtor asked me about running a paid workshop for her whole brokerage. I hadn't even published this episode yet.
