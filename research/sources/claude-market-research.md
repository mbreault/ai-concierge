## 1\. The eight findings that should change your sheet

**1\. "AI Concierge" is taken, and it means something else.** Across four differently-worded searches, not one human advisory service uses the name. What came back was hotel and hospitality chatbots (Sojern, Delight AI, Xyonix), a product literally named "AI Concierge" on [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/254399/AI-Concierge/), a CX glossary entry at [Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/glossary/ai-concierge) defining it as automated customer service, and an academic paper in the [Journal of Service Management](https://www.emerald.com/josm/article/35/6/136/1218595/AI-concierge-in-the-customer-journey) anchoring the term to the customer journey. The buyer who searches "AI concierge" wants software for their guests, not an advisor for their business. Details and alternatives in §2.

**2\. Your $1,000/month retainer sits in a structural dead zone.** The market is bimodal, and the gap is real: **$800–$1,900/month is leveraged group delivery** (office hours, clinics, shared cohorts) and **$3,500–$10,000/month is dedicated one-to-one.** Almost nothing transacts at $2,000–$3,000. You are selling dedicated 1:1 time at a group-tier price, and calling it "Enterprise" while doing it. Full price ladder in §3.1.

**3\. Every serious paid-discovery offer credits itself forward, with a clock.** This is near-universal and you don't have it. Erin Moore credits her $750 intensive to month one. Justin McKelvey credits $2,500 within 90 days. Density Labs credits within 60\. Treetop credits 100%. Karl Sakas — the agency consultant who popularized paid discovery — recommends crediting fully if the client moves within 2–6 weeks. **The deadline is the conversion mechanism**, and it costs you nothing.

**4\. Your $100 assessment isn't underpriced so much as under-delivered.** The published paid-discovery market clusters at $1,500–$2,500 (solo/SMB), $5,000–$7,500 (boutique), and $45,000+ (enterprise). What separates them is the artifact. Every credible one hands over a scored current-state map, a ranked opportunity list, a 30/60/90 with named owners, and a live readout. Thirty minutes cannot produce that. §3.2 has the full deliverable checklist that makes a discovery fee feel worth paying.

**5\. $15,000 for a half-day workshop is the most out-of-band number on your sheet — but the fix isn't a discount.** Published private half-days: [BlueShore.AI](https://blueshore.ai/business-training/) $999 (5 people), [Elansio](https://elansio.com/ai-training-cost-uk.html) £980 (20 people), [Data Trainers](https://www.datatrainersllc.com/pricing/) $2,500/day. The next rung up is [Marketing AI Institute](https://www.marketingaiinstitute.com/about/workshops) at **$75,000+**, which is brand-led. There is a published void between \~$8k and \~$75k that gets negotiated rather than listed — and it's filled by firms with a brand, not solo newcomers. Keep the $15,000. Change what it buys: a half-day *plus a shipped agent*. §5.

**6\. Your SLA is slower than the market floor and missing every protective clause.** 48-hour email is a P3 commitment. [DeployLabs](https://deploylabs.ca/fractional-ai-officer/) promises 24-hour email at $5,000/mo, [SEOKRU](https://www.seokru.com/pricing/monthly-retainer/) next-business-day at $1,800/mo. Market modal for P2 is **4 business hours**. You also have no severity tiers, no defined coverage window, no definition of "response," no exclusions, and no remedy. §4 is a full reference with quoted contract language.

**7\. Nobody in the market has contract language for AI-specific maintenance — and that's your wedge.** Model deprecations, API version breaks, prompt drift. It isn't a bug (the thing worked as specified) and it isn't a project (the client didn't ask for anything new). The standard [fix/update/new-release taxonomy](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/software-fixes-and-updates/_1) has no bucket for it, and I could not find a single published agreement that handles it. For a consultant whose entire pitch is *"and stay on if they want,"* naming and owning this category is the sharpest differentiator available. §4.4.

**8\. Copilot Studio pass-through costs are missing and will produce an awkward conversation.** Microsoft bills $0.01/message pay-as-you-go, $200/month for 25,000 credits, and a generative answer burns 2 credits while GenAI Voice burns 35 credits/minute (**$0.35/min**). At 125% of prepaid capacity, Microsoft *disables the custom agent*. There's also a large upside you should be quoting: **most features are no-charge for authenticated Microsoft 365 Copilot–licensed users**, which makes internal-facing agents dramatically cheaper to run. Full table in §3.4.

## 2\. The naming problem, and what the market actually calls this

### What "AI Concierge" currently returns

| Result | What it is |
| :---- | :---- |
| [Sojern AI Concierge](https://www.sojern.com/solutions/guest-experience/ai-concierge) | Hotel guest-experience chatbot |
| [Delight AI](https://delight.ai/), [Xyonix](https://www.xyonix.com/solutions/virtual-ai-concierge-solution) | Hospitality / healthcare virtual concierge products |
| [Capterra listing](https://www.capterra.com/p/254399/AI-Concierge/) | A software product literally named "AI Concierge" |
| [Lorikeet CX glossary](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/glossary/ai-concierge) | Defines the term as automated customer support |
| [Journal of Service Management](https://www.emerald.com/josm/article/35/6/136/1218595/AI-concierge-in-the-customer-journey) | Academic definition, customer-journey automation |
| [Ordaana](https://blog.ordaana.com/ai-concierge-services-how-businesses-can-offer-24-7-support-without-extra-staff/) | "24/7 support without extra staff" — SMB chatbot |

You'd be fighting an academic definition, a software listing, a CX vendor glossary, and hotel-tech SEO simultaneously. Both the semantics and the search intent point the wrong direction.

### What the market uses instead, by adoption

**Fractional CAIO / Chief AI Officer** is dominant. **Fractional AI Officer** (DeployLabs, Prime AI), **fractional Head of AI** (UK-leaning), **AI advisory retainer**, and **fractional AI Partner** (Plainpath) follow. Worth noting: *"concierge" does survive as a service-level adjective inside tier names* — it just isn't anyone's brand.

### Recommendation

Two moves that cost you nothing and keep what you like about the word:

1. **Name the sheet after what it does, not what you are.** Your own opening line is already the best copy on the page — *"find the workflow that hurts, get them using the tools, then build the thing that runs it — and stay on if they want."* That's **Find it. Build it. Run it.** Use it as the spine.  
2. **Move "Concierge" down one level, to the top retainer tier**, where it's doing honest work as a service level rather than a category claim. This is exactly how the word survives elsewhere in the market.

For anything that needs to be findable in search — a services page, a LinkedIn headline — use **"Fractional AI Lead"** or **"AI advisor on retainer."** Skip "Chief AI Officer": your buyers at $1M–$20M don't have chiefs, and the title reads as mismatched to the room.

One caution on your favor: "fractional" is consultant vocabulary. An owner-operator of a 40-person HVAC company doesn't search it. Use it in the metadata; say **"your AI guy, on retainer"** to the human.

**Benchmarks:** [Go Fractional](https://www.gofractional.com/insights/rates/chief-ai-officer) **\[B\]** puts CAIO hourly at median $200 (IQR $150–250) and models \~$5,700/mo — but discloses its sample is *7 job posts in 90 days*, so treat the retainer figure as modeled, not observed. [The AI Consulting Network](https://www.theaiconsultingnetwork.com/blog/ai-consulting-small-businesses-cost-how-it-works) **\[C\]** describes $2,000–$8,000/mo for 8–20 hrs at exactly your target segment ($1M–$20M revenue).

**Levers sellers use to tier, in order of frequency:** time volume → **response SLA** (the sharpest and cheapest differentiator; SEOKRU runs next-business-day → 4 business hours → 1 hour/7 days across three tiers) → meeting cadence → audience seniority (board-ready material is reserved for top tiers nearly everywhere) → ownership depth ("review" → "oversight" → "end-to-end ownership").

**Terms:** 3-month minimum is modal, 30 days' written notice standard. But "no minimum, cancel anytime" is *advertised as a selling feature* by Wingenious, DeployLabs and ChiefAIOfficer. Worth stealing: SEOKRU lets clients upgrade next cycle but makes downgrades wait out the minimum.

**Rollover is the least-standardized term and mostly doesn't exist.** SEOKRU is the *only* vendor publishing a policy: hours don't roll over, but **under 40% utilization triggers a free 90-minute strategy session.** That's a genuinely smart compromise — it kills the accruing-liability problem while defusing the "I paid for nothing" complaint. Nobody else addresses it publicly. Practitioner consensus ([Ben Guttmann](https://www.benguttmann.com/blog/please-never-include-rollover-hours-in-your-retainer-contract)) is to never offer rollover at all.

**Scarcity is priced in at the solo end.** Erin Moore caps at 8 active clients; Wingenious takes 2 new clients per quarter. Honest constraint, free urgency.

**The deliverable checklist.** Present in essentially every published assessment:

1. A **scored current-state artifact** — maturity score or workflow map  
2. A **prioritized opportunity list ranked by ROI × feasibility** — impact/effort matrix  
3. A **30/60/90 roadmap with named owners** (the numbers are nearly standardized)  
4. A **live readout call** — 60–90 min. Nobody just emails a PDF

What separates a $5,000 audit from a $1,500 one:

5. **A working prototype.** AdvantageWorks' single strongest differentiator — the buyer leaves with something that runs (with an explicit "this is not a production system" disclaimer)  
6. **Build-vs-buy analysis and a PoC brief** — the bridge artifact into implementation  
7. **A governance / data-handling section** — this is what makes it legible to whoever has to approve the spend  
8. **Named tools, not categories** — "Claude, specifically, for this" beats "an LLM"  
9. **A board-ready exec report with an explicit go/no-go** — this is the artifact that gets forwarded

**Format signals that carry weight:** fixed price \+ fixed scope \+ fixed timeline stated up front; a *written* document rather than a deck; explicit non-obligation language.

**How to price it.** Karl Sakas gives two rules that both hold up: price at **5–10% of the anticipated follow-on budget**, and **price below the buyer's purchasing-card limit** so it bypasses procurement entirely. That second rule is why $2,500–$5,000 is such a dominant price point — it isn't a coincidence.

**Conversion data is thin and mostly untrustworthy.** The one usable data point: [Sakas](https://sakasandcompany.com/start-using-paid-discovery/) reports of one client agency that "at least 90% of Phase 1 clients request the proposal, and 90% of those move forward" — a single named anecdote, directionally useful, statistically worthless. A widely-circulated claim that paid discovery lifts agency revenue \~60% traces to an unnamed survey **\[X\]**. ConsultKit's "35–40% better conversion" figures **\[X\]** come from a company selling software to AI consultants, with zero linked citations — and they're the upstream source for much of the 2026 SEO content on this topic.

**Read on your $500/head public workshop:** Iowa Tippie at $550 for a 4-hour virtual half-day is your closest comp and validates the number. > Firms that **only** train charge **£1,000+ per head** (4 of 6 specialists). Firms that **also implement** charge **£100–300** (2 of 5). Zero full-stack firms charge £1,000+.

Your instinct — *"Tools session makes them dangerous enough to care"* — is exactly right, and this data says price accordingly: cheap enough to fill, because the money is downstream.

| [Jitbit template](https://www.jitbit.com/helpdesk/sla/) **\[T\]** | 1 hr response / 3 hr resolution | 2h / 8h | 4h / 24h | 7h / 48h |
| [Law Insider clause](https://lawinsider.com/clause/guaranteed-response-time) **\[T\]** | 2 business hrs | 4 business hrs | 8 business hrs | 12 business hrs |
| [Digacore](https://digacore.com/blog/managed-it-service-level-agreement-sla/) **\[C\]** | 15 min / 4 hr resolution | 1 hr / 8 hr | 4 hr / 24–48 hr | 1 business day / 3–5 days |

**The convergence is real:** P1 \= 15 min to 2 hours (1 hour is the premium modal); **P2 \= 4 business hours, the single most common figure across every source type**; P3 \= 4 business hours to 1 business day; P4 \= 1–3 business days.

**"P1: 4 business hours, business hours only" is a defensible entry tier for a solo shop** — that's exactly Thirdlane Premium. Just don't call it 24/7. And don't promise 15 minutes: that's an MSP-with-a-NOC number.

### 4.2 Coverage windows and the clock-pause trap

[ScienceSoft](https://www.scnsoft.com/software-development/maintenance-and-support) **\[V\]** sells 8/5, 12/5, 12/7 and 24/7 as explicit purchasable options. Thirdlane's three tiers essentially *are* the coverage window — and note the precision: its 24x7 tier covers **Sev1 and Sev2 only**, leaving Sev3 at one business day. That's the right way to scope a small-shop after-hours offer.

**The trap to get right in writing:** on a business-hours SLA the clock pauses. A Friday 4:30pm P1 on a 4-business-hour SLA is legitimately due **Monday at noon** ([AltaTech](https://altatech.co/blog/it-support-sla-response-time/), [Securafy](https://blog.securafy.com/9-msp-sla-traps-that-still-cause-downtime-for-smbs)). Say so explicitly, or you will have that argument later, with a client, on a Friday.

**What 24/7 actually costs.** Two figures circulate and they're not contradictory: **\~15% uplift** ([ITBudgetCalculator](https://itbudgetcalculator.com/managed-services-cost) **\[B\]**) is the marginal cost of adding after-hours triage to a desk that already has a rotation. **\~2.3x** ([Technova](https://technovapartners.com/en/insights/ai-agents-implementation-costs-2026) **\[C\]**, €1,500–2,500/mo → €3,500–6,000/mo) is what a specialist firm charges when 24/7 means a named engineer and a real pager. **You are in the second regime.** Don't price 24/7 at a 15% uplift unless you genuinely want to carry the pager.

### 4.3 Define "response," or it will be defined for you

Securafy flags the common dodge: providers define "response" as *acknowledgment* rather than active work. The honest definitions:

- **SerenIT:** *"a qualified technician contacts the reporting user via phone, chat, or email with an initial diagnosis and expected resolution timeline"* — requires a diagnosis and an ETA, not a robot reply  
- **Thirdlane** uses "acknowledgment" throughout, and states *"Resolution targets are scoped during contracting based on environment, deployment model, and operational risk profile"*

**Thirdlane's posture is the right one for you: publish response times, deliberately decline to publish resolution times.** Also avoid *"best effort," "commercially reasonable,"* and *"reasonable time"* — unenforceable filler that reads as evasive.

### 4.4 Break/fix vs. new work — and the third category nobody has

**The canonical contract language** ([Law Insider, "Software Fixes and Updates"](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/software-fixes-and-updates/_1), from OEM agreements) **\[T\]**:

> **Fixes (covered):** *"bug fixes, critical patches, modified documentation or other changes intended to correct feature/function deficiencies"* **Updates / new releases (excluded):** changes that *"incorporate accumulated Fixes and new features"*

The operative phrase is **"intended to correct feature/function deficiencies."** If it never worked as specified, it's covered. If it works as specified and they now want it to do something else, it's new work. Lift that phrase verbatim.

**The argument to use when a client pushes back**, from [Morefield](https://morefield.com/blog/why-managed-service-providers-exclude-projects-from-their-agreements/) **\[C\]**:

> *"If MSPs tried to include all of that within the flat monthly fee, one of two things would happen: costs would spiral, forcing higher costs for technology support. Standards would erode, and projects would be rushed without proper planning."*

That framing — "a box with well-defined edges" — protects the client's interests, not just yours, which is why it lands.

**Now the gap.** [Galorath](https://galorath.com/blog/software-maintenance-costs/) **\[C\]** finds **enhancements are "50% or more" of maintenance spend** and adaptive maintenance another 25%. For an AI agent, "adaptive" means model deprecations, API version changes, and prompt drift — **work forced on you by third parties that is neither a bug nor a client request.** Neither standard bucket contains it, and I found no published contract language that handles it.

**Recommendation: name a third scope category.** Something like:

> **Platform Adaptation** — changes required to keep existing functionality working when a model, API, or platform changes underneath it. Covered up to N hours per month; billable beyond.

This is a drafting recommendation, not something I found in the market. That's the point: it's unclaimed, it's real, every client will eventually hit it, and it's the concrete answer to *"what am I paying $250/month for?"*

### 4.5 Uptime — don't offer it

[NinjaOne's MSA template contains no uptime commitment at all](https://www.ninjaone.com/blog/managed-services-agreement-terms-and-conditions-examples/). Thirdlane publishes none. ScienceSoft sells coverage windows instead of percentages. **Uptime clauses are normal for platform vendors and abnormal for implementation shops** — you don't control the substrate. If Anthropic or Microsoft is down, your agent is down.

If a client insists: 99.9% ≈ 8.7 hours downtime/year, 99.5% ≈ 43.8 hours. The **third-party-outage and scheduled-maintenance exclusions are load-bearing and non-negotiable**, and appear in essentially every published uptime clause.

### 4.6 Remedies

| Structure | Figure | Source |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Availability-banded credits | 99.5–99.9% → **5%**; 99.0–99.5% → **10%**; 95–99% → **25%**; \<95% → **50%**. **Capped at 30% of monthly fees** | [Flowie](https://get-flowie.com/sla) **\[V\]** — most complete published schedule found |
| Response-time miss | **20% of pro-rata monthly support fee**, stated as *"sole and exclusive remedy"* | [Law Insider](https://lawinsider.com/clause/guaranteed-response-time) **\[T\]** |
| Recommended MSP ladder | P1 miss \= 1 day's fee; sub-95% compliance \= 5% credit; repeated failures \= **30-day termination right without penalty** | [SerenIT](https://serenitllc.com/blog/msp-sla-guide) **\[C\]** |
| Recommended range | **5–20% of monthly fees**, and must be **automatic, not "upon request"** | [Digacore](https://digacore.com/blog/managed-it-service-level-agreement-sla/) **\[C\]** |

Credits are near-universally **capped** and near-universally declared the **"sole and exclusive remedy."** Never offer an SLA without both.

**Here's the opportunity:** both leading free MSP/helpdesk templates (NinjaOne, Jitbit) contain **no remedy clauses at all.** The small-shop default is response targets with no teeth. A modest, capped, *automatic* credit is a genuine differentiator at SMB scale — and it's cheap. **5% of a $250/month retainer is $12.50.** You will look more serious than firms charging ten times as much.

## 5\. Line-by-line against your draft

### What's already right, and rarer than you'd think

- **The narrative spine.** *"Sit with someone, find the workflow that hurts, get them using the tools, then build the thing that runs it — and stay on if they want."* Almost every competitor leads with a capability list. You lead with a mechanism. Don't let anyone edit this out.  
- **Paid discovery as step 1** — validated as the dominant market pattern, and the one most correlated with converting.  
- **"Tools session makes them dangerous enough to care."** This is the funnel insight the AI Enablement Insider survey proves with data: firms that implement underprice their training *because it's the funnel*. You arrived at it by instinct.  
- **"The first build reveals itself when someone elbows a coworker and says 'we could do this for our quoting process.'"** That's the actual mechanism of the sale, described better than any vendor page I read.  
- **Break/fix vs. new work already stated.** Most solo shops omit this entirely and then eat the difference.  
- **"The monthly slot where the next agent gets scoped."** Correct — the retainer's real job is the second sale. The price just doesn't reflect it yet.

### What to change

| \# | Item | Current | Issue | Recommendation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | **Name** | "AI Concierge" | Category collision with hospitality chatbots | Retitle "Find it. Build it. Run it." Move *Concierge* to the top retainer tier |

### One thing to be careful about

The revised ladder still has a lot of small-ticket products, and small tickets consume sales and scheduling overhead out of proportion to what they earn. A year of ten audits, five builds, and six retainers is real money, but it's also a lot of separate conversations for one person. The counterweight is the credit mechanism and the retainer cap — both exist to push each conversation up the ladder rather than sideways. Watch whether the free intro call becomes a time sink; if it does, put a $250 price on it and let the audit credit absorb it.

## 6\. Source quality — what not to cite

Being specific here because this topic is unusually polluted:

- **"$5K–$30K/month for fractional CAIO"** originates on a single [Iternal](https://iternal.ai/fractional-chief-ai-officer) page and is re-cited across the web without verification. Its apparent corroboration is an artifact of copying.  
- **"Momentum AI: from $2,500/month"** appears in two separate listicles. I fetched [their site](https://www.momentumhub.ai/fractional-chief-ai-officer/) — **no pricing is published at all.** Clean demonstration of the failure mode.  
- **"Gartner says 15–20% maintenance"** — the convention is real and traceable to [ScienceSoft](https://www.scnsoft.com/software-development/maintenance-and-support), but the **Gartner attribution is not verified anywhere.** Cite ScienceSoft, and say "of initial development cost," not "of license fee." Those are two different conventions (18–22% of net license fee is the Oracle/SAP enterprise standard, a different animal).  
- [**SVSB**](https://www.svsb.ai/blog/workshop-pricing) quotes AI half-days at $8,000–$15,000. SVSB is a **speaker bureau** and the article funnels to "Book a Speaker." Zero data.  
- [**ConsultKit**](https://www.consultkit.ai/blog/how-to-price-an-ai-readiness-assessment-what-the-market-actually-pays-in-2026-1773479248460) sells software to AI consultants; every citation is unlinked. It's the upstream contaminant for much of 2026's AI-pricing SEO.  
- **Every article titled "AI Agent Development Cost 2026"** from azilen, sparkouttech, devcom, braincuber and similar — excluded as content farms with invented ranges.  
- [**Go Fractional's**](https://www.gofractional.com/insights/rates/chief-ai-officer) **CAIO benchmark rests on 7 job posts in 90 days** — the page says so itself. The $200/hr median is directionally plausible; the $5,700/mo retainer is modeled, not observed.  
- **Azure's Copilot Studio pricing page renders per-credit price as "$-"** until you select a region. The $0.01 figure comes from Microsoft's blog. Re-verify before putting it in a contract.

The two genuinely well-methodologized sources in this whole report are [Techreviewer](https://techreviewer.co/research/software-development-company-rates-in-2026) (n=127, stated method) and [Danilina's AI Enablement benchmark](https://www.aienablementinsider.com/p/how-much-you-charge-says-a-lot-about-who-you-sell-to) (n=38, discloses its own skew, anonymizes firms). Everything else is vendor rate cards — which are fine, and are what most of this report is built on, but they're asking prices, not transacted ones.
